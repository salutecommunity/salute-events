About the film

Seat at the Table: South Asians’ Rise in American Politics follows South Asian political candidates and grassroots organizers through the 2024 election cycle—a historic moment for the community’s participation in American public life.

Filmed in an immersive documentary style, it takes viewers inside congressional and state-level campaigns and community organizing efforts. Alongside those individual journeys is a wider story of an immigrant community finding its political voice, confronting barriers, and reshaping what leadership in America can look like.

More about the documentary ↗

Nabeela Syed

State-level leadership / Illinois

The film follows Syed’s reelection campaign and her work on healthcare access, prescription drug affordability, and education.

Ravi Bhalla

Congressional campaign / New Jersey

Then mayor of Hoboken, Bhalla campaigns to become the first turban-wearing Sikh in the U.S. Congress.

Amish Shah

Congressional campaign / Arizona

An emergency-room doctor and state representative, Shah brings his focus on healthcare to a competitive congressional race.

Harini Krishnan

Grassroots organizing

Leading South Asians for Harris, Krishnan works to mobilize voters and volunteers in battleground states.

Neil Makhija

Local government / Pennsylvania

A Montgomery County commissioner and election official, Makhija’s story brings local governance and democratic participation into focus.

Descriptions reflect the people and campaigns depicted in the film’s 2024 election-cycle story, rather than a current officeholder directory.

Representation is
only the beginning.

Seeing South Asians in public office expands our sense of who belongs in leadership. But representation is not only about who holds a seat. It is also about whether our experiences and priorities help shape the decisions made there—from education and healthcare to economic opportunity.

That connection depends on civic participation. Voting gives us a voice in choosing our leaders. Learning about the issues and staying engaged between elections helps us hold them accountable. Together, these actions make representation more than a milestone. They make it an ongoing relationship between leaders and the communities they serve.

SALUTE’s work to expand access, visibility, and connection is rooted in that same belief: leadership matters most when it opens doors for others. Ahead of the midterm elections, Seat at the Table gives us a starting point for a conversation about how we each take part.

About the hosts.

SALUTE

SALUTE (South Asian Leaders Unite to Empower) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and curated private professional network focused on expanding access, visibility, and connection for South Asian women and allies.

We’re intentionally building a platform that elevates South Asian women’s leadership and connects people to relationships and opportunities—serving as connective tissue to the broader leadership ecosystem, rather than an insular network.

Visit SALUTE ↗

South Asian Trailblazers

South Asian Trailblazers is an award-winning media platform, community, and talent and advisory agency dedicated to elevating leading South Asians. Founded by Simi Shah, its mission is to amplify South Asian success so that generations of South Asians can have a meaningful line of sight to leaders who look like them.
