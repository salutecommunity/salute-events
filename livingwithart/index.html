Voice / 01

Kinjil
Mathur

Advisor and Board Member
Read bio +

Kinjil Mathur is an active participant in the arts, with a career at the intersection of culture and technology. Most recently, she served as Chief Marketing Officer of Squarespace, where over eight years she built and led the global marketing organization, scaling the company through its IPO and into its next chapter as a public company. Previously, she spent a decade in fashion and retail, helping established brands navigate the transformation to modern commerce.

Today, Kinjil brings her creative thinking and business acumen to the worlds she cares about, including her work with the NYC Public Housing Community Fund and Search for Common Ground, as well as an ambitious project with Sotheby’s that brought her deeper into the art world beyond collecting. She and her husband were featured in Artsy’s 2022 collector list.

Her work with culture and community has been recognized with honors including Adweek’s Visionary of the Year and Top 20 Tech-Savvy CMOs, Forbes’ CMO Next, Ad Age’s Women to Watch, Business Insider’s 27 Most Innovative CMOs, Gold House’s Gold100, and Vogue India’s 50 Most Influential Global Indians.

Photo: Diane Wah
Voice / 02

Sadaf
Padder

Independent curator and art advisor
Read bio +

Sadaf Padder is a Brooklyn-based independent curator and art advisor. Her curatorial practice explores migration, ecology, material histories, and cultural memory, with a focus on sustained support of artists and community engagement. As an art advisor, she has placed works, primarily by women artists, in private collections and institutions including the Baltimore Museum of Art, RISD Museum, and Northwestern University.

Padder serves on the boards of the Vera List Center and 12 Gates Arts and is a visiting critic at Columbia University. She was the 2026 Curator-in-Residence at Art Omi and has spoken at institutions including the Museum of Modern Art and Rutgers University. As a writer, she is a regular contributor to Artsy and Hyperallergic, writing criticism and artist features.

Photo: Grace Roselli, Pandora's BoxX Project
Voice / 03

Melissa
Joseph

Artist
Read bio +

Melissa Joseph is a New York based artist. She makes needle felted paintings about family, history, materiality, and place. She intentionally alludes to the labors of women as well as her experiences as the biracial daughter of an immigrant in rural America and the cultural mashups of diasporic life.

Her work has been shown in museums and galleries worldwide, and she actively participates in artist residencies such as Dora Maar House, Greenwich House Pottery, Dieu Donné Workspace Residency, The Textile Arts Center, Fountainhead, the Archie Bray Foundation for Ceramic Arts, and the Museum of Arts and Design.

Her work is in the permanent collections of the Akron Art Museum, Brooklyn Museum, Ruby City, the RISD Museum, ICA Miami, and the Flint Institute of Arts. She is the recipient of the 2025 UOVO Prize by the Brooklyn Museum and a 2026 NYFA/NYSCA fellow in Craft/Sculpture.

03 / The conversation
The Evening

From interest
to patronage.

Living with Art is for established patrons, emerging collectors, and those curious about building a more meaningful relationship with art.

It is a room for people who believe artists need more than an audience. They need informed advocates, sustained engagement, and people willing to invest in the lives of their work.

01

Why art, why now?

What feels important in contemporary art today

+

The evening opens with a fireside conversation about the work that feels urgent in contemporary art today.

Inside the conversation
  1. Where are curators finding artists whose work deserves greater attention?
  2. What challenges are artists facing today?
  3. What allows an artist to build a lasting practice?
02

How collecting begins

Finding work you love and trusting your own eye

+

A personal conversation explores how an interest in art can become an intentional collecting practice.

Inside the conversation
  1. How do you develop confidence in your own taste?
  2. Where do you begin if you have never collected before?
  3. How can a curator or advisor help you discover artists thoughtfully?
